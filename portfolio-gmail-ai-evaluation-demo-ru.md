# Russian Email AI Quality Review — Synthetic Portfolio Sample

> **Status:** synthetic demonstration created for portfolio use. It is not a real customer email, a real mailbox review, paid client work, or evidence of prior employment. No personal mailbox data was accessed.

## Task

Review an AI-generated Russian business email for meaning preservation, tone, clarity, completeness, and privacy. Decide whether it is ready to send.

## Source brief (fictional)

A company wants to tell a customer that payment for invoice No. 184 has been received, the order is planned to ship tomorrow, and ask whether documents can be sent electronically.

## AI-generated draft

> Привет! Деньги за счёт 184 получили. Завтра отправим. Документы скиньте электронно.

## Review result

**Decision: revise before sending.**

The draft is understandable, but it changes the meaning of the last sentence. The source asks whether the company can receive documents electronically; the draft instead tells the customer to send them electronically. It also uses an overly casual register for a routine business email and leaves the object of “отправим” implicit.

## Suggested revision

> Добрый день! Подтверждаем получение оплаты по счёту № 184. Отправку заказа планируем выполнить завтра. Подскажите, пожалуйста, можно ли направить документы в электронном виде?

## Evidence-based review

| Dimension | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning preservation | Needs correction | The question about receiving electronic documents became an instruction to send them |
| Tone and register | Needs correction | “Привет”, “деньги” and “скиньте” are too informal for this business context |
| Clarity | Needs correction | “Завтра отправим” does not explicitly say what will be sent |
| Grammar | Acceptable, but stylistically weak | The sentences are understandable; the main issue is register and precision |
| Privacy | No issue in this fictional example | No credentials or unnecessary personal data are requested |

## Reviewer checklist

1. Compare every sentence with the source brief; do not rely on fluency alone.
2. Preserve who is asking, who is acting, and whether a sentence is a question or an instruction.
3. Check register against the relationship and business context.
4. Replace vague references with explicit nouns when they improve clarity.
5. Flag missing details instead of inventing them.
6. Do not copy real customer names, invoice details, or private mailbox content into a portfolio.

## Scope and limitations

This sample demonstrates a short Russian-language email review. It does not establish professional translation credentials, native-speaker status, Gmail/Google Workspace usage volume, or experience with real client mailboxes. A real evaluation must follow the client's rubric and privacy requirements.
